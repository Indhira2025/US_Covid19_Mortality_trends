## Overview
This project analyzes the evolution of COVID-19 mortality in the United States from January 2020 to April 2025. Using CDC and NCHS data, it evaluates confirmed COVID-19 deaths, excess mortality, hospitalization trends, vaccination coverage, and variant waves to understand the transition from pandemic outbreak to endemic management.

## 1. Problem / Purpose

The COVID-19 pandemic evolved through multiple variant-driven waves, each differing in transmissibility, severity, immunity levels, and public health response. Reported COVID-19 deaths alone do not fully capture the pandemic’s total impact.

This project aims to:

- Analyze temporal trends in COVID-19 mortality across variant waves  
- Estimate excess mortality relative to pre-pandemic baselines  
- Evaluate changes in case fatality rates (CFR) and hospitalization metrics  
- Assess geographic disparities in mortality  
- Examine the relationship between vaccination coverage and excess deaths  
- Quantify the contribution of pneumonia and influenza to excess mortality  

---

## 2. Methodology

**Data Sources**
- CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)  
- NCHS (National Center for Health Statistics)  
- Weekly state-level mortality data (Jan 2020 – Apr 2025)  
- Hospitalization and vaccination coverage data  

**Analytical Approach**
- Defined variant waves using CDC classifications  
- Calculated rolling averages, CFR, and hospitalizations per 1,000 cases  
- Estimated excess deaths using pre-pandemic mortality baselines  
- Performed regression analyses to assess effects of COVID-19, pneumonia, influenza, and vaccination rates on excess mortality  
- Compared per capita mortality across U.S. regions  

---

## 3. Conclusion

- Mortality peaked during the Original and Delta waves  
- CFR and severity declined across subsequent waves, especially during Omicron  
- Excess deaths often exceeded reported COVID-19 deaths, particularly in early 2020  
- Southern and Western states showed higher per capita mortality  
- Pneumonia and influenza contributed significantly to excess mortality  
- Higher vaccination coverage was associated with lower excess deaths  

The findings reflect a shift from high-severity pandemic phases toward endemic management, while highlighting persistent regional disparities.The report  file for this analysis is " Report.pdf "

## 4. Impact

This project provides:

- A longitudinal assessment of COVID-19 mortality trends  
- Evidence of vaccination’s protective population-level impact  
- Insight into regional health inequities  
- A framework for evaluating future infectious disease waves  
- Support for data-driven, equitable public health strategies
